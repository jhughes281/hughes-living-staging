Staging Agreement

What you and Hughes Living agree to

Version

Draft This wording has not yet been reviewed by an attorney. It describes how the service works today and may change before Hughes Living takes its first paid staging. If it changes, you will be asked to accept the new version.

This agreement is between you (the person or business holding the account) and Hughes Living Staging (“Hughes Living”, “we”). It applies to every house you stage through your account. It is written to be read; the short version of each section is its heading.

1. The furniture is ours, and it is for sale

Every piece placed in a house you are staging belongs to Hughes Living until a buyer has paid for it in full. Staging a house does not rent, lend or transfer ownership of any piece to you, the property owner, the seller, the listing agent or the brokerage.

Each piece carries a price and a tag. You agree to leave the tags and the QR card in place while the house is staged, and not to sell, give away, lend out or remove any piece yourself.

2. Getting it in and getting it out

You choose pieces from what is shown as free in the warehouse. A piece is only yours to stage with once it is placed in one of your houses in your account. Hughes Living sets the staging fee for each house and confirms the day the furniture goes in and the day it comes out.

You confirm that you have the right to let us into the house on those days, or that the person who does has agreed. You will tell us about stairs, gates, parking limits and anything else that affects moving furniture safely.

3. Looking after the furniture

While furniture is in a house you are staging, you are responsible for keeping it in the condition it arrived in, apart from ordinary wear from showings. That means keeping it indoors in the room it was placed in, away from pets, smoking, food and drink, moisture and direct damage, and not using it as everyday household furniture unless we have agreed to that in writing.

If a piece is lost, stolen, or damaged beyond ordinary wear while it is in the house, tell us straight away. You agree to pay the reasonable cost of repair, or the piece’s tag price if it cannot be repaired or is not returned. We will show you the damage and the cost before charging anything.

You are responsible for making sure the house is insured in the usual way for a property being sold. Our furniture being there does not make us responsible for the house, its contents or anyone visiting it.

4. How a sale works

A buyer who scans the QR code can order a single piece, order every available piece in the house, or ask a question. An order is a request to Hughes Living. It is not a contract with you and it is not a payment.

Hughes Living is the seller of every piece. We contact the buyer, agree the sale, take the payment and arrange collection or delivery. You do not take money for furniture, quote prices other than the tag price, or promise a buyer a piece, a discount or a delivery date on our behalf.

A piece that is being held or has sold may stay in the house until the staging ends or until we collect it, at our choice. We keep buyers’ names, phone numbers and email addresses private; your account shows that an order was made and its status, not who to call.

5. Sales tax

Because Hughes Living is the seller, Hughes Living charges the buyer any sales tax due on the furniture and is responsible for reporting and paying it. You are not selling furniture under this agreement and should not collect tax on it. The staging fee is a separate charge to you; if tax applies to it, it will be shown on your invoice.

6. The staging fee and the sales credit

The staging fee for each house is set by Hughes Living and shown on that house in your account before the furniture goes in.

When a piece sells out of a house you are staging, % of the price it sold for is credited against that house’s staging fee. The credit is earned when the buyer has paid in full and the piece is marked sold. It is a reduction of the fee, not a commission, wage or payment to you; it applies only to the house the piece sold from; and it cannot reduce a fee below $0. If a sale is refunded, its credit is reversed.

If you are a real estate licence holder, you are responsible for any disclosure to, or consent from, your client or broker that your licence rules require for a credit that benefits you.

7. Other services

Carpet cleaning and any other service offered in your account is a request, not a booking. The price shown is an estimate from that provider’s posted rates. The work is done by the provider named, who is responsible for it; the final price is confirmed before the work is done.

8. Ending a staging

Either of us can end a staging by telling the other. When a staging ends, you give us access to collect every piece that has not sold, and the buyer page for that house closes. The staging fee already earned, and any charge under section 3, remain payable.

9. Your account

You are responsible for what is done through your account and for keeping its password to yourself. Enter only houses you are actually staging and only details you are entitled to share. We may suspend an account that is misused.

10. The limits of what we promise

We do not promise that staging will sell a house, sell it faster or raise its price, or that any piece will sell. To the extent the law allows, neither of us is liable to the other for indirect or consequential losses, and our total liability to you for a house is limited to the staging fee you paid for it. Nothing here limits liability that cannot be limited by law.

11. Changes, and which law applies

If this agreement changes, the version above changes and your account will ask you to accept the new one before you continue. Stagings already under way finish under the version you had accepted when the furniture went in, unless you agree otherwise. This agreement is governed by the laws of the State of Texas.
